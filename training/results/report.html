On-policy distillation · Qwen3.5-397B → Qwen3.5-2B · TPC-H (DuckDB)

A 2B model that writes analytical SQL, taught by a 397B teacher

The teacher (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) solves all 22 TPC-H questions. With on-policy distillation and no training on the benchmark, the 2B student goes from 10/22 to 19/22 by majority vote, and from 4.3 to 14.9 on a single answer. It also improves on questions it has never seen and on a second benchmark, Spider.

What was done

  • Method. The student writes its own answers (with thinking, temperature 1.0). The teacher scores every token of those answers, and the student is trained to match the teacher's per-token distribution (reverse KL). The teacher's scores come from Tinker. The student trains with prime-rl on one H100 on Modal: a LoRA of rank 32 at 512 answers per step.
  • Training questions. Two sources: about 18k Spider and BIRD questions on other databases, plus about 900 new questions on the TPC-H schema, written and answered by the teacher. Anything resembling a test question was removed, both by text and by SQL structure.
  • Held-out tests. Four sets, none of them trained on:
    • The 22 TPC-H questions.
    • 30 new questions I wrote on the TPC-H schema (“fresh”).
    • 22 near-copies of the TPC-H questions with the meaning changed (“probes”, which catch memorization).
    • 300 Spider test questions on databases never seen in training.
  • Scoring. A query counts as correct only if it runs in DuckDB and returns the same rows as the reference.

Three ways the score went up

  • Distillation itself. The 2B gained about 10 TPC-H points on a single answer. Fresh and probe scores rose by similar amounts, so this is general SQL skill, not recall of the 22.
  • Harder training questions, at a gentle learning rate. I added about 170 teacher-written multi-step analytical questions. At a learning rate of 5e-5 they added about 1 point on top of the plain run.
  • Spending more at answer time, with no extra training. Two changes:
    • Retrying after a SQL error with only the error message removes almost every syntax and binding failure.
    • Sampling 16 answers and keeping the most common result adds 3–5 questions on TPC-H.
    For the 4B, one answer gets 17.5/22; voting gets 19; retries plus voting gets 20.

  • The TPC-H gain comes from the data, not from extra steps. Forty more steps of clean training leave TPC-H flat (6.2 → 6.5). Every near-test variant adds 7–10 TPC-H points.
  • Most of the gain needs no memorization. Training on the 22 query shapes with new constants gets within about 3 points of training on the exact 22, and Spider holds or improves.
  • The model learns shapes, not wording. Rewording the 22 helped least at step 190, though it caught up by step 210 (14.8). The probes, which keep the shapes but change the meaning, rose almost as much as TPC-H.
  • Pure memorization has a real cost and an expiry date. Training on only the 22 prompts gives the highest TPC-H score but drops Spider by 7 points. Twenty steps later the run destabilized and every score fell, the 22 included.

These runs train on the test by design. They are shown only as this trade-off curve and are never reported as benchmark results. The chart compares every run at step 190. At step 210, new constants reach 15.1 and rewording 14.8, both with Spider at 66–69%. The exact 22 with clean data reaches 13.1. The control's step 210 is pending.

What broke, and the fixes

  • Thinking that loops on hard questions. At a learning rate of 2e-4 on the hard questions, the 2B began to loop in its thinking. The share of answers cut off at the 6k-token limit rose from 10% to over 60%, and TPC-H fell to 4/22. Cutting the learning rate to 5e-5 kept it stable. The cut-off rate is now the early warning on every run.
  • Untied weights. prime-rl's Qwen3.5 trainer never re-ties the output layer to the input embeddings. Training only started working from checkpoints prepared with them untied.
  • Slow teacher scoring. The teacher scored answers one at a time, so steps took 7–10 minutes. Scoring them concurrently cut that to about 1.5–4 minutes at the same batch size.

Caveats

  • I picked the best 2B checkpoint partly by looking at test scores. A stricter protocol would pick it on the held-out dev set and score the test only once.
  • The 2B is not yet at 22/22. The remaining misses are reasoning errors on the hardest multi-step queries, not SQL dialect errors.
  • Scores are at temperature 1.0 with thinking on. Majority votes over 16 answers are noisy at the ±2-question level.
